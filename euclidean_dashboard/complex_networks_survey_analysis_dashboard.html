Mathematical Formulation & Network Metric Foundations

Rigorous formulation of student similarity topology and centrality metrics

1. Vectorial Euclidean Metric & Adjacency

Each student $u$ is represented as a Likert response vector $\mathbf{x}_u \in \{1, 2, 3, 4, 5\}^D$, where $D$ is the dimension of the selected feature subspace ($D = 60$ for all questions, or $D = 15$ for individual topics). The pairwise distance $d(u, v)$ is the Euclidean norm:

$$d(u, v) = \|\mathbf{x}_u - \mathbf{x}_v\|_2 = \sqrt{\sum_{k=1}^{D} (x_{u,k} - x_{v,k})^2}$$

An undirected edge is established between students $u$ and $v$ if and only if their vectorial distance does not exceed the threshold $\theta$:

$$A_{uv} = \begin{cases} 1 & \text{if } u \neq v \text{ and } d(u, v) \le \theta \\ 0 & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}$$

2. Node Centrality Formulations

• Node Degree ($k_i$):

Total number of students with whom student $i$ shares high opinion concordance:

$$k_i = \sum_{j=1}^{N} A_{ij}$$
• Watts-Strogatz Local Clustering Coefficient ($C_i$):

Ratio of edges between neighbors of node $i$ over maximum possible edges:

$$C_i = \begin{cases} \frac{2 e_i}{k_i(k_i - 1)} = \frac{\sum_{j, m} A_{ij} A_{im} A_{jm}}{k_i (k_i - 1)} & \text{if } k_i \ge 2 \\ 0 & \text{if } k_i < 2 \end{cases}$$

Mean clustering over the entire cohort is $\langle C \rangle = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N C_i$.

• Closeness Centrality ($C_C(i)$ — Wasserman-Faust formulation):

Generalized to disconnected graphs by scaling with reachable component size $n_c = |\mathcal{C}_i|$:

$$C_C(i) = \left(\frac{n_c - 1}{N - 1}\right) \cdot \frac{n_c - 1}{\sum_{j \in \mathcal{C}_i, j \neq i} d_G(i, j)}$$
• Normalized Betweenness Centrality ($C_B(v)$ — Brandes Algorithm):

Measures how often student $v$ lies on shortest geodesic paths between other student pairs:

$$C_B(v) = \frac{2}{(N-1)(N-2)} \sum_{s \neq v \neq t} \frac{\sigma_{st}(v)}{\sigma_{st}}$$
• Average Path Length ($\langle L \rangle$ on Giant Component $\mathcal{C}_{\max}$):
$$\langle L \rangle = \frac{1}{|\mathcal{C}_{\max}|(|\mathcal{C}_{\max}| - 1)} \sum_{i \neq j \in \mathcal{C}_{\max}} d_G(i, j)$$

3. Theoretical Rationale for Recommended Threshold ($\theta^*$)

In complex network theory, random and geometric graphs undergo a percolation phase transition when the average degree exceeds the Molloy-Reed criterion:

$$\kappa = \frac{\langle k^2 \rangle}{\langle k \rangle} > 2 \quad \text{or for Poisson-like graphs } \langle k \rangle \ge 1$$

For the 60-question survey dataset:

  • Sub-critical regime ($\theta < 6.0$): Graph is severely shattered into disconnected dyads, triads, and $>45$ isolated nodes ($|\mathcal{C}_{\max}| < 20\%$).
  • Critical percolation ($\theta^* \approx 7.00$): A cohesive giant component emerges ($\approx 80\text{--}88\%$ cohort inclusion) while distinct opinion cliques remain cleanly discernible. Average per-question divergence satisfies: $$\bar{\Delta} = \sqrt{\frac{\theta^2}{D}} = \sqrt{\frac{49}{60}} \approx 0.903\text{ Likert steps}$$ ensuring connected students differ by less than 1 Likert point on average.
  • Super-critical / Clustered regime ($\theta > 9.0$): Graph over-saturates toward a trivial quasi-clique ($\langle k \rangle > 35$), washing out modular community structure.
